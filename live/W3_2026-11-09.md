## 2. Refill ahead by burn rate

**Inputs (the meter).** The meter adds no react per payment:
- **Heartbeat re-reads.** The wallet's `verify(K_EPOCH)` answer now carries today's unspent and spent. The heartbeat is that re-read, so the meter rides on traffic that already exists.
- **LowWater.** A LowWater also counts as a reading (spent = held − unspent), but only while the books are exact for that chain: no unanswered grant, no open reclaim, no pending return. Right after a STOP roll, `held` still holds the drained allowance; a reading then would invent a burst (V10 shows it).
- **Ordering.** Readings apply only in origin-time order, so duplicates, replays and late readings change nothing.
- **Rate.** The rate is the spend over a window of at least 10 min (heartbeat / 4).

**Decision.** `_refillAhead`, step 7 of `_poke`, runs after serving and after the `frozen` return. For each chain:
1. **Only fast chains.** The chain must burn a chunk (cap/10) within `reclaimLead`. Slower chains stay on the W2 path, because a chunk-sized top-up would park budget on them for hours. This was found in the simulation.
2. **Skip if not asking.** A chain projected dry that isn't asking gets nothing: its agent has slowed or stopped.
3. **Read first.** It acts only on a reading younger than `refillLead / 4`. If the reading is older, it asks for one; the answer is its own react seconds later. This stops a burst that has ended from getting another hour of its old rate.
4. **Top up.** With fewer than `refillLead` = 20 min of spending left, it tops up from the pool by `refillHorizon` = 1 h of the rate (at least one chunk).
5. **Start the donor path.** With fewer than `reclaimLead` = 90 min left and a pool that can't give that top-up, it sends one reclaim per react. The donor is the chain with the most slack beyond its floor + one chunk + its own projected need (rate × 2.5 h). The donor's reading must be fresh too: a chain whose agent just got busy has no rate yet.

**Why these numbers** (from the live ones):
- The top-up takes 2 s, so it is never on a payment's path if it goes out on the last react before the chain runs dry. 20 min is one 17.66-min Cron period plus margin.
- The donor path takes 30 min plus up to two Cron periods, about 65 min. 90 min is that plus the 20-min top-up point plus margin.

**Safety.** Every grant and reclaim goes through `_grant` / `_sendReclaim`. The pool is debited first, there is at most one unanswered grant per chain, grants carry the epoch, and nothing runs while frozen. So the cap invariant and the STOP and epoch rules are the same code as before. The fuzz now also checks:
- invariant **(f)**: Σ over wallets of allowance (unspent + spent) ≤ the owner's cap, for today and tomorrow, at every step;
- a `burst` action, so busy agents drive the meter;
- `test_coverageWalkWithBursts`, a second seeded walk. The original walk is unchanged, so its known kills stay.

**Gas.** The worst react is 518,967 gas (asserted ≤ 600k). Refill ahead on every chain at once is 439,773.

### Share of payments that had to wait (`test/RefillAhead.t.sol`, simulated day, 30 days per profile)

The setup:
- production timing (waits 30/30/16 min, Cron 17.74 min);
- every queued message delivered each minute, so a top-up shows as a 1-min wait (2 s live);
- a warm-up day first, so the measured day has pre-grants and yesterday's meters;
- agents keep their calls in order and retry each minute.

Cap $20, three chains. Each profile ran on the same seeds with refill ahead off (W2) and on (W3 as deployed, mode 1), and with refill ahead but no idle beat (mode 2):

| Profile | Calls paid | Waited: W2 → W3 | Share waited: W2 → W3 (mode 2) | Waits ≥ 20 min | Wait-minutes | Still waiting at midnight |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| burst ($7/h on chain 0, 01:00–02:30) | 1317 / 1314 | 63 → 21 | **4.78 % → 1.59 %** (1.29 %) | 4 → 1 | 441 → 102 | 88 → 91 |
| night batch ($2.5/h, 01:00–05:00) | 1315 / 1319 | 8 → 12 | 0.61 % → 0.91 % (0.76 %) | 0 → 3 | 23 → 119 | 77 → 73 |
| steady ($0.7/h all day) | 1038 / 1041 | 19 → 20 | 1.83 % → 1.92 % (1.63 %) | 1 → 3 | 88 → 147 | 136 → 133 |

- **Counting the calls still waiting at midnight.** "Still waiting at midnight" is mostly budget stuck on other chains' $2 floors. Counted as waits too, the share goes 10.7 % → 8.0 % (burst), 6.1 % → 6.1 % (night batch), 13.2 % → 13.0 % (steady).
- **Reacts and callbacks per 30 days**, W2 → W3:
  - burst: 8627 → 8381 reacts and 3182 → 3035 callbacks;
  - night batch: 8375 → 8282 and 3156 → 3165;
  - steady: 7086 → 6908 and 2938 → 2825.

  Refill ahead adds reads and reclaims; the idle beat saves more than that at night.

**Reading the table:**
- Refill ahead cuts waiting where W2 is weak: a burst outruns the $1 low-water cushion, and W2's donor path starts too late.
- On gentler days, W2's LowWater-plus-partial-serve trigger already rarely waits, and W3 is a wash within noise. The added long waits on those days are calls that W2 left stuck until midnight; with those counted, the shares match.
- The idle beat (mode 1 vs 2) slightly delays the meter after a quiet night. The table reports the deployed mode 1.
- In-suite assertions, default 4 days per profile:
  - burst: W3 at least halves the waited calls and the wait-minutes, and adds no long wait;
  - the other profiles: waited + still-waiting is no worse than W2 + seeds/2.

## 4. P2-2 idle cost: decision

Fee per react = price × (gas + 100k). At 100 gwei, 100k gas = 0.01 lREACT. Gas figures:
- **Live:** idle tick 93–115k (median 115k); tick with a beat 149–179k, so about +49k per beat; Cron 1,059.5 s, which is 81.5 ticks a day.
- **Foundry** (`test_gas_idleReacts`): heartbeat-answer react on W2 50.6k (one chain, about 61k on two). On W3 the meter adds work: 73.4k steady, 100k for the first reading, 114k when it wakes from idle.

Idle day, two chains (the deployment):
- W2: 54.4 beats a day;
- 120-min beat: one every 7 ticks = 123.6 min, so 23.3 a day;
- `test_idleDayCostsFewerBeats` on three chains: 81 → 33 beats, 165 → 117 reacts.

| Option | lREACT / idle day | Two weeks | Lost STOP, nobody spending | Lost STOP, agents spending |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W2: beat 40 min | 1.75 ticks + 1.11 beats = **2.86** (live 2.9) | 40.0 | 46–53 min | 46 min |
| Flat 120-min beat (gate option A) | 1.75 + 0.48 = **2.23** | 31.2 | ~123–135 min | ~123–135 min, while other agents keep spending (up to the day's cap) |
| Cron only while retries are pending (gate option B) | ≈ 0 while asleep | ≈ 0 + wakes | **unbounded** until an origin event or someone calls `reannounce()` | depends on a wake event |
| **Chosen: 40 min while active, 120 min after 2 h without spending** | 1.75 + 0.52 = **2.27** | **31.8** | worst 123 min at P = 17.74 (bound ceil(120/P)·P + 2); nothing is spent meanwhile | worst **46 min**, as before; ≤ 40 min + P + 2 after the first spend (worst 42) |

- **Why option B is rejected.** It needs a sleep/wake mechanism: subscribing to Cron is done from the RN instance, not from `react()`, so it needs a self-callback on Lasna, which is unverified live. It also stops pre-grants and the meter, and leaves a lost STOP with no bound.
- **Why the chosen option.** It keeps option A's saving on idle days (most of the judging window) and keeps the W2 bound whenever an agent is spending, which is when a lost STOP costs money.
- **Active days cost a little more:** about +0.1 lREACT a day, because the meter makes the answer react heavier, plus refill-ahead reads. The simulated days above still show 1–3 % fewer reacts overall, thanks to the idle nights.
- **Residual risk.** An agent that wakes and spends only what it already holds, with no Short or LowWater, is noticed at the next idle beat (≤ ~126 min). The owner's UI can call `reannounce()` at any time.
- **Not done:** P2-3. Billing on the gas limit isn't shown, so the limit stays at 700k.

Tests:

  - active: 46 at every P;
  - idle: 134 / 123 / 128 / 128 / 131 / 135.
- `test_lostStopAfterAnIdleStretchFollowsTheFirstSpend` (worst 42 min).
- `test_idleDayCostsFewerBeats`.
